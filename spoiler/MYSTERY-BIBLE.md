## Content note, non-spoiling

A missing overnight host, workplace pressure, a family disagreement, and people who lie or are mistaken. No violence, injury, or death. Reading is untimed. Sound is optional and local.

## What happened

Mara Ellison walked out of WNBR on her own during the Monday 12 October 2026 overnight. She was not hurt, not taken, and not dead. She refused to air an interview with her sister. She faded the board to a near-silent storm bed at 2:17 so the silence sensor would not page anyone, recorded the reason on an unplayed cart at 2:04, and badged out the south dock at 2:19 with her card and PIN. Her car was gone by 6:00. The other three vehicles were still there.

This is a walk-off. It is not a homicide, an abduction, or a medical collapse.

## Solution evidence

The account that holds:

- Person: Mara Ellison.
- Act: she left on her own and was not hurt.
- Motive: she would not air a private segment about her sister. The motive is established by the cart, and the rider explains the business pressure. Motive is required.

A raw proof is the hold cart plus the dock reader, or the hold cart plus the parking checklist. Order does not matter. A third already-logged note may ride along.

The voluntary deduction is that same proof. Once it is in the notebook, an accusation may cite that deduction plus any second notebook entry. The deduction already contains the cart and a physical corroborator.

The cart is the only record of choice and of being unhurt. The dock and the lot are the physical corroborators. Requiring the cart is not an arbitrary gate. No other note states that she chose to leave and was not hurt. Badge plus lot without the cart proves a credential and a car, not a choice. That rejection is intentional.

## Red herrings

The blank camera looks like a covered-up crime. It is Reed avoiding a fault writeup. The badge log is independent.

Dennis's first alibi and the personal fee make him look like someone who would hide a disappearance. He wanted her on the air, not gone, and he was on the roof.

Jules's lie about going home, plus the sponsor fight, looks like guilt. Jules was asleep and says so when shown the couch.

## Failure classes

- Wrong theory: person, act, or motive does not match. Notes the player attached are quoted against that account when they conflict.
- Insufficient: the right person, act, and motive, with at least one relevant note, but not a full proof.
- Lucky guess: the right person, act, and motive, with only unrelated notes such as the couch and the camera.
- Supported: the right trio plus a full proof. The case stays open after every failure.
