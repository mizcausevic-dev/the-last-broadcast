# SPOILERS: Mystery bible

This document reveals the solution. It is not shown in the game.

## What actually happened

No one was hurt and no one was taken. June Pell, night engineer at WKXR 88.7 in Marrow Creek, left the building on foot through the loading dock shortly before midnight on the station's last night on air.

She left because station manager Ellis Voss had prepared a substitute public file. The real binder logs three spots as "community calendar" notices and has unpaid invoices clipped to them. The printout in the archive tray, stamped 12:06 AM, relabels those spots as paid and drops the invoices. June dubbed a copy of the real file, wrote a note to the archivist, and walked the copy out to Harbor Light inspector Priya Shah, who had come to the dock early. June did not trust the lobby. Ellis was there.

The disappearance is the time she is off the property delivering that copy. Her car stays in the lot because she rode in Priya's shuttle. Her keys stay on the peg.

## Was a crime committed?

Not a violent one. Ellis wrote a midnight sign-off June did not make, and he prepared a public file that changes the ad log. That is the wrongful conduct. Nadia Crowe scripted a thank-you caller because the phones were dead. That breaks station honesty and does not explain the exit. Cal Renner's pledge short is a Tuesday bookkeeping miss with the envelope still sealed. It is not a theft tonight.

## What the player is explaining

The form is an explanation, not a murder charge.

A successful explanation has to establish all of this:

- June left the station on her own.
- She left on foot by the loading dock.
- The point was to get the real public file to the inspector.
- The conduct that made the secret exit necessary was Ellis's substitute file.
- The three attached notebook entries actually cover both the exit and the file contradiction.

A motive essay is not required. The purpose field is the explanation. Naming Ellis's desire to protect the sale is optional color, supported by his lobby conversation, and is not a separate required control.

## Why the ending follows

Every fact in the ending is on a page, a reel, a door strip, or in a statement the player can open before filing.

- The booth log stops at 11:12 in her hand. The midnight line is someone else's.
- The board shows an orderly pause and a two-minute call to the Harbor Light inspector at 11:16.
- The reel is a finished public-file copy labeled 11:21.
- The note, 11:22, says she is leaving by the dock with the copy because the lobby is Ellis, and that the shelf binder is the real file.
- The front door has no exit cycle after Ellis's 10:40 entry. Her keys are still on the peg. Her coat is not.
- The dock latch is taped "CAL, LEAVE FOR J". Priya Shah's courtesy card is in the rail.
- Cal, if asked after the latch is found, saw her walk out talking, with a reel box, and shut the door after the shuttle.
- The binder and the 12:06 printout disagree on the same three spots.
- Nadia's pad and Cal's Tuesday envelope are dated and worded as different problems.

The ending does not add a phone call, a confession from offstage, or a document the player never saw.

## Timeline

- 10:40 PM. Ellis badges in the front door. He does not badge out.
- About 11:00. June asks Cal to tape the dock latch. She is ducking the lobby, not a goodbye speech. Cal does not know about the file.
- 11:12. June's log stops: "music bed, dub copy, back for sign-off."
- 11:16. Station line calls the Harbor Light inspector for two minutes.
- 11:21. The dub reel is full.
- 11:22. June writes the archive note and leaves the real binder on the shelf.
- Before midnight. Priya is at the bottom of the dock ramp. June walks out with the reel box, alive, talking. Cal presses the tape back down after the shuttle leaves.
- 12:00. Ellis writes a clean sign-off June did not perform.
- 12:06 AM. The office printer stamps the substitute file. Ellis leaves it in the tray. He cannot find a blank where the binder was, because June did not take the binder. She took the copy.
- About 1:15. Nadia comes back in. She had gone to her car after the music bed. She tore the scripted caller sheet because she thought the dub was about her show.
- 1:40. Rowan Hale, weekend archivist and the second name on June's emergency card, arrives. Ellis called. The dawn transfer inspection is the stake. There is no countdown.

## Suspects

### Ellis Voss, station manager, in the lobby

- Motive: get a clean public file in front of the buyer so the transfer holds.
- Opportunity: he is inside the whole time. Interior doors are not on the front-door strip, so he can reach the booth and the office printer.
- Knowledge: he knows the printout and the sign-off are his. He does not know June met Priya. He did not watch her leave.
- Account: he has been in the lobby since 10:40, she never came through the front, the printout changes nothing, she told him to sign off if she got busy, the transmitter key is missing, and if money is wrong Cal is the person to ask.
- True: badge time, she did not use the front door, he cares about the sale.
- Incomplete: he does not mention that the printout relabels the ads.
- Deliberate lies: the printout "changes nothing"; June authorized the sign-off; the transmitter key is missing.
- Mistaken or deflecting: keys on the peg mean she is still inside. He points at the roof, the tower, and the pledge box.

### Nadia Crowe, evening host, in the booth

- Motive: avoid having a scripted caller heard. It is embarrassment and a policy breach, not a plan to move June.
- Opportunity: she was in the booth at the start of the music bed. She then went to her car. She was not at the dock.
- Knowledge: she heard June say she had to dub something. She saw the archive key in June's hand. She did not see June leave the building.
- Account: June killed the mic, ran the long bed, and went to dub. Nadia snapped at her to leave the board alone. They did not fight.
- True: the music bed, the dub, the archive key, the torn sheet, the dead phones, the scripted line.
- Mistaken: she thought the dub was about her show.
- Not a lie about the exit: she says she did not see June leave. That is true.

### Cal Renner, transmitter tech, at the dock

- Motive: none for the disappearance. He wants the Tuesday memo believed.
- Opportunity: he has the dock and the rack. He taped the latch because June asked.
- Knowledge: he saw June and a woman in a Harbor Light coat. He heard the name Priya. He does not know what the two public files say.
- Account: she asked him to leave the latch. He thought it was about Ellis's goodbye speech. Later he saw her walk out with a reel box and shut the door after.
- True: the tape, the request, the witness account, the Tuesday seal.
- Incomplete until asked about the dock guest: the ungated topic does not name Priya.
- The pledge answer is true.

## Evidence that supports the solution

- `ev-booth-log`, `ev-board`, `ev-reel`: she planned a copy and a return, and she called the inspector.
- `ev-note`: her plan, in her hand, naming the dock, Priya, the binder, and the tray.
- `ev-lock`, `ev-hook`: not the front door, not her car.
- `ev-dock`: tape for J, Priya's card, door secured after a guest.
- `stmt-cal-saw`: eyewitness, gated behind `ev-dock`.
- `ev-binder`, `ev-printout`: the contradiction.
- `stmt-ellis-file`, `stmt-ellis-signoff`: his minimizing account, contradicted by the pages.

## Alternatives, and what rules them out

- She is still inside, or she went to the tower. Every staff room is open. The transmitter key is on its peg (`ev-txkey`). The note says she left. Cal saw her leave. Absence of a body is not the proof. The proof is the exit record.
- She was taken. The board is an orderly pause. The note is a plan written before the exit. Cal saw her walking and talking, not being held.
- She left by the front door or in her car. The lock strip has no exit cycle. The keys are on the peg. The coat is gone.
- Nadia silenced her over the caller. The pad is Nadia's hand. Nadia says the dub was not her show, and the reel label agrees. Nadia never claims to have seen the dock.
- Cal robbed the pledge box and June caught him. The envelope is sealed, dated Tuesday, and explains a $40 misfile. Nothing in it is from tonight.
- Ellis physically removed her. He had a motive to alter paper, and he lied about that paper. The door strip and the dock witness do not put a struggle in his hands. His conduct is the file, not an abduction.
- The note is a plant. The hand matches the log lines above 11:12, including the hooked J, which the player is told as an archive observation, not as outside graphology.

## Minimum facts for a supported explanation

The player does not need every item.

Required on every successful path:

- `ev-binder` and `ev-printout` (the file deduction, or both pages attached with the exit already bundled).
- `ev-dock` (every exit combination uses the latch, or uses Cal's witness, and the witness topic requires the latch).

Plus one exit corroboration:

- `ev-note`, or
- `stmt-cal-saw`.

Then at least one bundling deduction if the raw pages cannot fit in three attachment slots:

- `ded-file-swap` or `ded-dock-exit` (both is the clean path).

Not required: booth log, board, reel, scratch pad, pledge memo, coat pegs, door strip, transmitter key, Nadia's confession, the "side problems" deduction, the "not the front door" deduction. Those rule out alternatives or make the exit easier. Tests omit them and still finish.

## Solution is not a single wording trick

The note, the latch, the card, and Cal's statement agree about the route. The binder and the printout disagree in labels and in time, not in a pun. Ellis's "same facts" line is optional color once both pages are read.

## Content note (player-facing, not a spoiler)

A person is missing at the start. The story has workplace dishonesty and a deliberate absence. It does not depict violence, injury, or death.
